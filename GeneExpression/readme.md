Research Paper Title:LCE: An Open Web Portal to Explore Gene Expression and Clinical Associations in Lung Cancer (2019)

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6477796/

Problem Statement: Lung Cancer Gene Expression Analysis Optimization

Consider a lung cancer gene expression analysis system with:

Two sample categories (Normal Lung Tissue and Lung Cancer Tissue).

Five input parameters (Gene Expression Level, Fold Change, P-value, Adjusted P-value, and Gene Variance).

Multiple gene expression samples collected from patients.

Thousands of genes available for analysis.

Different gene expression levels across normal and cancerous tissues.

Seven analysis stages (Data Collection, Preprocessing, Normalization, Differential Expression Analysis, Feature Selection, Classification, and Evaluation).

Two gene expression patterns (Upregulated Genes and Downregulated Genes).

Multiple machine learning algorithms (Random Forest, Support Vector Machine, and Logistic Regression).

Each gene has a specific expression value in every sample.

Gene expression levels vary depending on the biological characteristics of the samples.

Statistical parameters influence the identification of significant genes.

The system must identify important genes associated with lung cancer.

Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO) is used to select the optimal subset of genes while reducing unnecessary features.

The classification model must distinguish normal and cancerous lung tissue samples accurately.

Objective: Minimize the number of selected genes and classification error while maximizing lung cancer classification performance and identifying significant gene expression biomarkers.
